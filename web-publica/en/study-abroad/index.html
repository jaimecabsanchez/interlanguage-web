The experience

Much more than learning English

Living and studying in another country changes how you see things. This is what it can bring, each at their own pace:

  • ConfidenceHandling everyday life in another language and setting.
  • IndependenceGetting organised, making decisions and taking on age-appropriate responsibilities.
  • TalentsDiscovering or developing interests: sport, music, art, science…
  • ImmersionEnglish stops being a subject and becomes the language of every day.
  • An international outlookLiving alongside classmates from other places and cultures.

Length of stay

A term or a full school year?

A school term

  • A first international experience
  • Language and cultural immersion
  • Less time away from home
  • Availability depends on the school and destination

A full school year

  • More time to settle in
  • Academic continuity throughout the year
  • Full participation in school life
  • Independence that grows step by step

Two terms are also possible, depending on the school. We decide the length together, with the year they will return to in mind.

Frequently asked questions

What families usually ask us

What level of English do they need?

It depends on the school and destination. Some ask for a minimum level or set a test; others offer English support. We assess it when we get to know the student.

What is the best age to study abroad?

There is no ideal age for everyone. Their maturity, their English and the stage of their studies all matter, and each school sets its own admission ages. It is worth thinking from the start about the year they will return to.

What if they find it hard to settle in?

The first days are usually the hardest. We prepare for it before departure and, during the stay, we follow up as agreed with you.

What is the difference between boarding and a host family?

At a boarding school they live at school, with other students and boarding staff. With a host family they live in a home and attend a day school. Each option suits different students, and not every option exists in every destination.

How do you choose the schools?

From what you tell us and what we learn about the student: age, level, interests, personality and your priorities. We present a few options and explain why each one fits.

What is a guardian?

An adult in the country who acts as the student’s point of contact with the school, for example for outings or emergencies. Some schools require one, and in Canada under-17s travelling without their parents need a custodian. If needed, we help you arrange it.

Will they be safe and well looked after?

We explain how each school looks after its students and who will be their point of contact there.

Will we be able to talk to them often?

Yes. Each school has its own rules on phones and times; we tell you about them before you choose.

When should we start planning?

Ideally a school year in advance: places can run out. If you ask later, we check which options are left.

What paperwork is needed?

It depends on the destination and the school: the admission application, reports from their current school and, in some cases, a visa and insurance. We tell you what each one requires.

What happens when they come back to Spain?

We plan it before choosing a school: which year they will do abroad and which year they will join on their return, so the experience fits into their education.

Will their studies need to be recognised?

No recognition is needed to join any year of Primary or ESO (compulsory secondary). For Bachillerato and other studies that require the ESO certificate, studies done abroad must be officially recognised. We review each case. Official information (Spanish Ministry of Education)

What budget should we plan for?

It depends on the destination, the school, the length of stay and the accommodation. We prepare a personalised budget that sets out what is included and what is paid separately: travel, transfers or English support, for example.

What does your advice include?

The first consultation is free and with no commitment. If we go ahead, we set out in writing what our work includes, its fees and its terms before we start.
